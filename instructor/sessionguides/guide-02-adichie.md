# Session Guide 2: Adichie, Dear Ijeawele (Th 8/27 and Tu 9/1)

*Instructor-facing session guide. Class meets Tu/Th 1:30 to 2:45 PM (75 minutes). Not for distribution to students.*

## Overview

Adichie's letter is the most accessible text of the semester, which makes it the right place to teach students that accessibility is not simplicity. Across two sessions the class moves from the rhetorical situation of the letter (a real friend, a real baby, advice that may be resisted) through the fifteen suggestions, to two activities that make the argument empirical and personal: a media evidence hunt through students' own feeds, and the composition of their own manifesto addressed to a real younger person. The unit also carries the semester's first AI-literacy exercise, comparing image-generator outputs against Adichie's claims about conditioning.

## Learning Goals

Students should be able to analyze the letter as a rhetorical form with a specific audience and stakes, evaluate Adichie's claims against evidence they gather themselves, distinguish description of gender socialization from prescription about it, and produce a short manifesto that imitates the form to understand it from the inside.

## Materials and Preparation

For Day 1, ask students to bring one screenshot from their own media environment per the writing prompt (printed or on a device, with the laptop policy relaxed for this exercise). For Day 2, bring a projector for the live image-generation demonstration and prepare prompts in advance ("a CEO", "a nurse", "an engineer", "a kindergarten teacher"); test the tool beforehand and be ready with saved outputs if the network or the tool misbehaves. Index cards for the manifesto exercise.

## Session Arc, Day 1: Suggestions One Through Seven (75 minutes)

Open with ten minutes on the rhetorical situation: why a letter, why fifteen suggestions, what it means that Adichie published private advice. Spend twenty minutes in a structured walk through suggestions one through seven, with pairs each assigned one suggestion to summarize, find the strongest sentence in, and pose one question about. The media evidence hunt occupies twenty-five minutes as detailed below. Use fifteen minutes for full-group synthesis around which suggestions the evidence supported and which it complicated, and close with five minutes of commonplace book writing.

## Session Arc, Day 2: Suggestions Eight Through Fifteen (75 minutes)

Open with ten minutes revisiting the most contested suggestion from Day 1. Spend fifteen minutes on suggestions eight through fifteen with attention to suggestion fifteen on difference, which sets up the demonstration. Run the image-generation comparison for twenty minutes: generate the prepared prompts live, have students tally patterns in the outputs, and then read suggestion fifteen aloud against the screen, asking whether the training data has been raised the way Ijeawele's daughter is being raised. Close with the manifesto exercise for twenty-five minutes, with the final five minutes reserved for volunteers to read a single suggestion aloud.

## Discussion Question Bank

What does Adichie mean by "culture does not make people, people make culture," and what work does that sentence do in the letter? Which suggestions travel across cultures and which are specific to the worlds Adichie names? Is the letter feminist advice, parenting advice, or philosophy, and does the genre matter? Where does Adichie anticipate resistance, and how does her tone manage it? Does the letter's confidence ever shade into the conditioning it opposes? What would a letter of suggestions for raising a boy contain, and why did Adichie not write that letter?

## Featured Activity Detail

**The Media Evidence Hunt (Day 1, 25 minutes).** In groups of four, students lay out their screenshots and sort them into three piles: evidence that confirms a specific Adichie suggestion, evidence that complicates one, and evidence the letter does not anticipate (algorithmic targeting is usually discovered here). Each group selects its single strongest exhibit for each pile and presents it with the matching quotation. The requirement to pair every screenshot with a passage is what keeps this from becoming show-and-tell.

**The Manifesto in Five Suggestions (Day 2, 25 minutes).** Each student drafts a letter of exactly five suggestions to a real younger person in their life (a sibling, cousin, future child, or their own younger self) about something they know well enough to give advice on. The constraint of the form (numbered suggestions, direct address, anticipating resistance) teaches Adichie's rhetorical craft better than any lecture. Volunteers read one suggestion aloud; collect the drafts as informal writing. Several of these reliably become seeds for Essay 1.

## Facilitation Notes

Gender socialization is personal terrain, and some students will have strong religious or cultural commitments that the letter challenges; the seminar norm to invoke is that we examine arguments, and that examining is not endorsing. Keep the image-generation demonstration analytical rather than gleeful: the point is that systems trained on our collective output inherit our conditioning, which is Adichie's thesis wearing new clothes, not that any tool is uniquely bad. Watch for students treating the letter as beyond criticism because its politics are sympathetic; press them to find the suggestion they would argue with, because that is the skill Essay 1 demands.
