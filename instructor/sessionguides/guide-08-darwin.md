# Session Guide 8: Darwin (Tu 11/3 and Th 11/5)

*Instructor-facing session guide. Class meets Tu/Th 1:30 to 2:45 PM (75 minutes). Not for distribution to students.*

## Overview

Darwin enters the course not as a science lesson but as one of the most consequential pieces of argumentation ever published, and the unit reads him for how he argues: the patient accumulation, the disarming concessions, the rhetoric of a man who knows exactly what his idea will cost his readers. Day 1 makes natural selection mechanically vivid through the selection simulation and close reading of Darwin's argumentative craft. Day 2 follows descent with modification out of biology, through the meme-evolution exercise, and confronts the cautionary history of Social Darwinism as the semester's sharpest lesson in how scientific authority gets misappropriated. Essay 2 final drafts are due Day 2.

## Learning Goals

Students should be able to explain variation, inheritance, and selection as a mechanism rather than a slogan, analyze Darwin's rhetorical strategies for delivering an unwelcome idea, evaluate where the evolutionary analogy illuminates cultural change and where it misleads, and explain what Social Darwinism got wrong as a matter of both science and ethics.

## Materials and Preparation

Day 1 needs the selection simulation kit: a large bag of mixed dried beans (or colored candies) and squares of patterned fabric or printed backgrounds, plus tally sheets; rehearse the rounds so the mechanics run in under twenty minutes. Day 2 needs the meme-lineage examples: choose one meme format with a traceable multi-year history and print or project four or five generations of it. Prepare a short, factual summary of Social Darwinism's history with primary quotations, chosen carefully.

## Session Arc, Day 1: The Mechanism and the Rhetoric (75 minutes)

Open with ten minutes on what students think natural selection says, collected without correction onto the board; the misconceptions (progress, intention, "survival of the fittest" as slogan) become the day's targets. Run the selection simulation for twenty-five minutes as detailed below. Spend twenty-five minutes in close reading of the assigned selections for Darwin's craft: where he concedes difficulties before critics can raise them, where he reaches for domestic breeding as the familiar lever, and where his language betrays that he knows the stakes. Close with ten minutes revisiting the opening board and crossing out what the simulation and the text have corrected, and five minutes of commonplace writing.

## Session Arc, Day 2: Descent Beyond Biology (75 minutes)

Open with ten minutes on the fossil record problem and arguing from incomplete evidence, calling back explicitly to Sappho's fragments. Run the meme phylogeny exercise for twenty-five minutes as detailed below. Spend twenty-five minutes on the misappropriation history: what Social Darwinism claimed, why it fails as science (selection has no moral direction) and as ethics (is does not make ought), and what this teaches about every era's temptation to launder its prejudices through its most prestigious science, with the question of AI hype invited but not forced. Reserve ten minutes for Essay 2 final-draft collection and synthesis, and five for exit writing.

## Discussion Question Bank

What three ingredients does selection actually require, and which is doing the work in Darwin's argument? Why does Darwin open with pigeons? Where does Darwin concede the most, and is the concession sincere or strategic? Does natural selection have a direction, and why do readers keep hearing progress in it? What survives in a population, and what survives in a culture, and are those the same kind of surviving? What exactly was Social Darwinism's error, and could we be making its equivalent now? What does Darwin's account ask of its hearers, in the Genesis gallery walk's sense?

**Protocol option: Four A's rounds.** The Day 1 close reading of Darwin's craft, or the Day 2 misappropriation discussion, can be run as Four A's rounds: groups of four mark the selections silently for the four A's (Darwin's assumptions, what they agree with, what they want to argue with, what they aspire to — his handling of objections is a frequent and useful "aspire"), then share in rounds, one "A" per round, every point anchored in a cited passage; an open round on implications and a short debrief close it. Reach for this when the room splits over evolution or goes quiet under it: the rounds give students with religious reservations a structured way in ("argue" is a sanctioned round, not a confrontation), and the "agree" round keeps the skeptics reading before ruling. Citation: Gray, J., "Four 'A's Text Protocol," National School Reform Faculty (NSRF), https://www.nsrfharmony.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/FourAsTextProtocol-N.pdf.

## Featured Activity Detail

**The Selection Simulation (Day 1, 25 minutes).** Groups scatter a counted, mixed population of beans across a patterned background; each student forages as a predator for a timed round, picking beans one at a time by sight. Survivors "reproduce" (each surviving color doubles), and the rounds repeat. Tallies across three generations show camouflaged colors climbing toward fixation, and no one intended anything: the population changed because variation existed, some variants were eaten less, and the survivors' traits were inherited. Debrief on exactly which parts of the metaphor are load-bearing and which are toys, a calibration exercise in analogy that the whole course trades on.

**The Meme Phylogeny (Day 2, 25 minutes).** Project the prepared generations of one meme format and have groups construct its family tree: identify the variations between generations, the selection pressures (what makes a variant get shared), and the inheritance mechanism (the template). The exercise is rigorous fun, and the rigor arrives in the second half: groups must identify one place where the analogy misleads, such as the fact that memes are intentionally designed, that "fitness" here means engagement rather than survival, and that the selectors include recommendation algorithms, which connects back to the Allegory unit's puppeteers. Close by noting Dawkins coined "meme" precisely to make this analogy, and ask whether the internet vindicated or vandalized it.

## Facilitation Notes

Some students arrive with religious reservations about evolution, and the unit's framing carries them: we are reading how Darwin argues and what his idea does in culture, an enterprise open to every metaphysics in the room, and the Genesis unit's both-and posture should be invoked by name. The Social Darwinism segment requires factual sobriety; let the primary quotations do the indicting and keep the lesson epistemic, that prestige science gets conscripted by power, rather than partisan. Day 2 collects Essay 2 finals, so expect fatigue and let the meme exercise supply the energy.
