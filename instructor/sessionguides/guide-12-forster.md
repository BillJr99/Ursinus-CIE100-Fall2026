# Session Guide 12: Forster, The Machine Stops (Th 12/3 and Tu 12/8)

*Instructor-facing session guide. Class meets Tu/Th 1:30 to 2:45 PM (75 minutes). Not for distribution to students.*

## Overview

The course ends where it began, underground: Forster's buried cells answer Plato's cave across the whole semester, and students who were promised that return in August should be reminded of it now. The unit reads the 1909 story as the syllabus's last and strangest text, a prophecy that anticipated video calls, content feeds, and machine dependence by a century, and uses it to convene every prior text for a closing argument about what the students will do, the fourth core question. Day 1 reads the story and runs the prediction audit and the personal Machine mapping; Day 2 (the Tuesday that follows a Thursday schedule) harvests the voluntary app-fast reports, runs the design-fiction finale, and holds the Essay 3 revision workshop. The Writing Fellow reflection is due Day 2.

## Learning Goals

Students should be able to analyze the story's anticipations of contemporary technology without reducing it to a prediction scorecard, trace Vashti's losses and explain why the story makes them invisible to her, connect Forster to Plato, McLuhan, and Coates in a genuine synthesis, and articulate, in their own design fiction, what they take the semester's warning and hope to be.

## Materials and Preparation

Day 1 needs the prediction audit handout (a two column sheet: passage from the story, its 2026 counterpart) and board space for the Machine mapping. Day 2 needs the fast reports (assigned as the voluntary option in the writing prompt), the design-fiction prompt slips, and students' Essay 3 drafts for the closing workshop. Bring the cave image from week one and put it up without comment; someone will notice, and that moment is worth protecting.

## Session Arc, Day 1: Vashti's World (Th 12/3, 75 minutes)

Open with ten minutes on the publication date, 1909, letting the impossibility of it register: before radio broadcasting, Forster furnished a room with a video plate, a global network, and a lecture-feed culture. Run the prediction audit for twenty minutes as detailed below. Spend twenty-five minutes in close reading of Vashti: what she has lost (touch, surprise, the surface of the earth, unmediated experience) and the story's craft in making her unable to mourn losses she cannot perceive, with the explicit Plato connection that she is a prisoner who decorates her cave. Devote fifteen minutes to Kuno, the body's advocate, connecting his insistence that "man is the measure" back to Coates's embodiment. Close with five minutes of commonplace writing and a reminder of the voluntary 24 hour app fast before Tuesday.

## Session Arc, Day 2: The Machine Stops (Tu 12/8, 75 minutes)

Open with fifteen minutes of fast reports from volunteers, harvested for patterns rather than confessions: what was noticed, what was hard, what was unexpectedly easy. Spend fifteen minutes on the ending, read partly aloud: the stopping, the touch in the dark, the airship glimpse of the untainted sky, and the question of whether the final pages are despair or hope. Run the design-fiction finale for twenty-five minutes as detailed below. Reserve fifteen minutes for the Essay 3 revision workshop in pairs, and five minutes for the course's last exit writing: one sentence each on the four core questions, sealed in an envelope addressed to themselves for CIE 200.

## Discussion Question Bank

How did Forster see it coming, and what was visible in 1909 that made this extrapolation possible? What is the Machine's message, in McLuhan's sense, and why does its content (lectures, music, ideas) disguise it? Why has direct experience become vulgar in Vashti's world, and is secondhand knowledge accumulating a similar prestige in ours? Is the Mending Apparatus's failure a malfunction or the story's theology? What does Kuno mean by "the Machine stops," and what is the difference between his knowledge and Vashti's information? Is the ending hopeful, and would hope be earned? After Plato, Coates, McLuhan, and Forster: what will you do?

## Featured Activity Detail

**The Prediction Audit (Day 1, 20 minutes).** Pairs work through the handout matching passages to their contemporary counterparts: the plate to the video call, the lecture feed to content platforms, the dislike of direct experience to mediated everything, the Committee to the platform, the Book to the help system no one questions. The audit is deliberately easy, and its debrief is where the work happens: the class must articulate why the predictions landed, and the strongest answer, that Forster extrapolated not technologies but appetites (comfort, mediation, the outsourcing of effort), reframes the story from prophecy into diagnosis, which is what makes it usable for the design fiction on Day 2.

**The Personal Machine Map (Day 1, woven into close reading).** On the board, the class builds Vashti's dependency stack (food, air, communication, ideas, medicine, music) and then, beside it, a 2026 student's stack. The instruction is precision: not "technology" but named systems, and for each, the honest answer to Kuno's question, what happens if it stops, and who in the room knows how it works. The map should be left up for Day 2.

**The Design-Fiction Finale (Day 2, 25 minutes).** Each student receives a prompt slip: write the opening paragraph of "The Machine Stops, 2126," a version of the story for two centuries after Forster, choosing what the Machine has become and what their Kuno notices first. Ten minutes of writing, then small group readings, then volunteers aloud. The exercise sends students out of the course as authors of the warning rather than recipients of it, and the strongest paragraphs reliably braid the semester: caves, dreams, feeds, and machines. Remember that students have been building "The Machine Question" strand of their commonplace books all semester ([/Participation/MachineQuestion](/Participation/MachineQuestion)), and this design-fiction finale is its culminating entry, "The Machine Stops, 2126," so direct them back to their semester of sightings as raw material. Collect the paragraphs with the commonplace books; together they are the course's real final artifact alongside Essay 3.

## Facilitation Notes

The app-fast reports must remain voluntary and unscored in tone as well as fact; students who did not attempt it should have full standing in the harvest discussion. Resist the temptation to end the course on technological pessimism: Forster's ending touches hands in the dark and glimpses the sky, and the design fiction should be framed as inheritance, not doom. The Tuesday session follows a Thursday schedule and is the last meeting; protect the closing letter exercise's five minutes, since the sealed envelope returning in CIE 200 is the kind of long game this course exists to play. Essay 3 final drafts are due during finals week by the announced deadline.

## Connections Forward

The course ends, and the forward connection is the students themselves: the sealed letters travel to CIE 200, the commonplace books go home, and the four questions remain open on purpose. A closing word to the room that the texts were never the curriculum, the conversation was, sends them out the way CIE intends.
