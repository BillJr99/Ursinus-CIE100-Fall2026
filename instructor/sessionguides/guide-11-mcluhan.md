# Session Guide 11: McLuhan (Tu 11/24 and Tu 12/1)

*Instructor-facing session guide. Class meets Tu/Th 1:30 to 2:45 PM (75 minutes). Not for distribution to students.*

## Overview

McLuhan supplies the theory for what the whole semester has been circling: that the forms of our media remake us beneath the level of their content, which is why the course could read feeds through Plato, image generators through Adichie, and hearing formats through McPhee. The unit's two sessions are split by Thanksgiving break, which the design turns into an asset: the media inventory begins before break and reports after. Day 1 establishes the medium-is-the-message claim against its best objections and runs the one-story-three-media lab; Day 2 (after break) runs the production exercise and the hot-cool sorting game, ending on the question the course has been saving: where a conversational AI sits in McLuhan's scheme. Essay 3 first drafts are due Day 2.

## Learning Goals

Students should be able to state McLuhan's central claim precisely and distinguish it from vaguer media criticism, analyze how form shapes content across platforms they use daily, produce the same content in multiple media and articulate what each form forced, and apply (and strain) McLuhan's categories on new media he never saw.

## Materials and Preparation

Day 1 needs one current news story prepared in four forms: a short vertical video, a social thread, a print article, and a podcast segment, all covering the same event, queued for projection and audio. Day 2 needs the hot-cool sorting cards (a deck of media old and new: radio, lecture, comic, group chat, livestream, voice assistant, conversational AI, and so on) and devices for the production exercise, with the laptop policy suspended for the day. Assign the break inventory at the end of Day 1.

## Session Arc, Day 1: The Claim (Tu 11/24, 75 minutes)

Open with ten minutes on the claim itself, stated three ways until it is precise: the medium is the message because the change of scale, pace, and pattern a medium introduces matters more than anything it carries. Spend fifteen minutes on the strongest objections, invited from students and supplemented (surely content matters; the claim seems unfalsifiable), because McLuhan survives the objections better when they are stated at full strength. Run the one-story-three-media lab for thirty-five minutes as detailed below. Close with ten minutes assigning the Thanksgiving media inventory (a log, not a fast: students record which media they and their families use across the holiday and what each medium does to the gathering), and five minutes of commonplace writing.

## Session Arc, Day 2: The Environment (Tu 12/1, 75 minutes)

Open with fifteen minutes of inventory reports from break, harvesting patterns onto the board: the television as hearth, the group chat as parallel gathering, the phone at the table. Run the production exercise for twenty-five minutes as detailed below. Spend twenty minutes on the hot-cool sorting game, ending deliberately on the conversational AI card and letting the class discover that it resists the scheme (it is a medium that talks back, whose content is generated by the medium itself), which is the course's AI-literacy capstone question rather than an answer. Reserve ten minutes for Essay 3 first-draft collection and a preview of Forster, and five for exit writing.

## Discussion Question Bank

What exactly does McLuhan mean by message, if not content? Is "the content of a medium is always another medium" an insight or a riddle? If a medium reshapes us regardless of content, what is the moral status of producing content at all? What did the printed book train its readers' bodies and attention to do, and what does the feed train? Are McLuhan's hot and cool categories load-bearing or just fun? Where does a system that generates its own content sit in a theory built on channels that carry it? Is McLuhan a determinist, and is he right to be?

**Protocol option: Four A's rounds.** The Day 1 objections segment converts cleanly to Four A's rounds, and the protocol suits McLuhan unusually well because the class already splits into delighters and distrusters: groups of four mark the reading silently for the four A's (McLuhan's assumptions, what they agree with, what they want to argue with, what they aspire to — the aphorisms supply the "aspire" round on their own), then share in rounds, one "A" per round, each point citing a specific passage; close with an open round on implications and a brief debrief. Use it when the room is polarized between the two camps or quiet because nobody trusts the prose enough to commit; the required citations do the precision work the unit needs, forcing "vibes" back into sentences on the page. Citation: Gray, J., "Four 'A's Text Protocol," National School Reform Faculty (NSRF), https://www.nsrfharmony.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/FourAsTextProtocol-N.pdf.

## Featured Activity Detail

**One Story, Three Media (Day 1, 35 minutes).** The class examines the same news event in four prepared forms: vertical video, social thread, print article, and podcast. Groups each take one form and complete a structural audit: what does this form require of the story (length, emotional register, what must come first), what does it make impossible, what does it train its audience to expect from all news? Groups report, and the board fills with a table whose punchline assembles itself: the event was constant, and everything else varied with the container. The debrief names the McLuhanite conclusion and then tests it: did any content survive all four forms intact, and is that residue what we should call the news?

**The Production Exercise (Day 2, 25 minutes).** Groups take one finding from the semester (a discussable claim from any course text) and produce it in two media of different temperature: for example, a thirty second vertical video and four sentences of print. The constraint is the curriculum: students feel, in their own editing choices, the form making demands the content must obey, which converts McLuhan's claim from assertion into experience. Each group shows both versions and names one thing the form forced. The exercise doubles as review, since the claims being produced are the semester's own ideas, now visibly reshaped by their containers, which is itself the lesson.

## Facilitation Notes

McLuhan's prose is aphoristic and students either delight in it or distrust it; channel both reactions into the precision work, because the unit fails if the claim stays vibes. The break-split schedule means Day 2 carries real re-entry load alongside a draft deadline; the inventory report-out is deliberately low-effort and high-interest for that reason. Keep the AI question genuinely open at the end of Day 2; the course's integrity on AI topics rests on the instructor not supplying the answer, and Forster will dramatize the stakes two days later.

## Connections Forward

Forster is the unit McLuhan was preparing: a 1909 story in which the medium has become the world. The handoff question for the reading: Vashti's Machine carries lectures, music, and conversation, so by McLuhan's lights, what is its message, and why does only Kuno hear it?
